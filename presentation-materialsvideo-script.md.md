# Video Script / Outline
**Target length: 2:30–3:00** | Solo presentation — Hung Hao Huang delivers the whole script. Break it into natural pauses/cuts if it helps with recording (e.g., record each section separately and edit together), but it should read as one continuous presentation.

### [0:00–0:20] Hook + Topic Intro
> "How many AI models do you think launched last month? Not last year — last month. The answer is: a lot more than you'd guess. I'm Hung Hao Huang, and today I'm talking about the 2026 AI model release race — and why it's changing how people and companies use AI."

### [0:20–1:00] The Concrete Example
> "Let's make this real. On July 24th, 2026, Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 — and here's the wild part: it was their fourth major Claude model release in under two months. Opus 5 costs exactly the same as the model it replaced, but roughly doubled its score on Anthropic's own benchmark suite. It's not even their smartest model — that's still a separate model called Fable 5 — but Anthropic is betting most people don't need the smartest model, they need the best value.
>
> And it's not just Anthropic. OpenAI rolled out GPT-5.6 on July 9th. xAI shipped Grok 4.5 the very next day. This is happening across the entire industry, practically every few weeks."

### [1:00–1:40] The Bigger Pattern
> "So why is this happening? A year ago, AI companies competed by releasing one huge 'best model' every so often. Now every company sells a whole *ladder* of models — cheap and fast at the bottom, frontier and expensive at the top — the same way phone companies sell budget, mid-range, and flagship phones. The competition isn't just 'who's smartest' anymore, it's 'who gives you the most capability per dollar.'
>
> There's also a safety angle: some companies are choosing *not* to release their most powerful models to the public at all — Anthropic has a model called Mythos that's restricted because of how good it is at finding security vulnerabilities."

### [1:40–2:15] Why It Matters to Society
> "This affects real people. Faster, cheaper models can mean AI gets more accessible to schools, small businesses, and individual developers. But it also means the 'best' tool changes every few weeks, which makes it genuinely hard to keep up — even for someone researching this alone, like me. And it raises a fair question: are companies safety-testing models as carefully when they're shipping this fast?"

### [2:15–2:45] Wrap-up + Discussion Question
> "So here's what I want you to think about: [Discussion Question — see below]. Thanks for watching!"

**Discussion Question (must also appear in your README.md):**
> "If a genuinely better, cheaper AI model can appear every few weeks, how should people and companies decide when it's actually worth switching — and does that pace make AI systems easier or harder to trust?"
