## Topic Summary
Since late 2025, major AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI, and others) have shifted from occasional "flagship" model launches to shipping new, tiered models every few weeks. Our project looks at this pattern through a concrete recent example — Anthropic's release of Claude Opus 5 on July 24, 2026, its fourth Claude 5-series model release in under two months — and asks what this faster pace means for cost, access, and public trust in AI.

## Key Findings
- Anthropic released **Claude Opus 5** on July 24, 2026: same price as its predecessor, but roughly double the benchmark performance, and close to (but not matching) the intelligence of Anthropic's flagship model, Fable 5.
- OpenAI (GPT-5.6, July 9, 2026) and xAI (Grok 4.5, July 8, 2026) shipped major releases within weeks of Opus 5 — this is an industry-wide pattern, not one company.
- AI companies increasingly compete on **price-per-performance** and release *tiers* of models (budget → flagship) rather than one single "best" model.
- Some companies are deliberately restricting their most capable models from public release for safety reasons (e.g., Anthropic's non-public "Mythos" tier).
- Faster releases raise real questions about decision fatigue, cost planning, and whether safety testing is keeping pace with shipping speed.
